## What the wind numbers can and cannot support

**Result (MERRA-2, native cell centred 23.0 N 54.375 E, 2000–2024, no missing hours):** DP ≈ 73 VU/yr, **low** in Fryberger's classes. RDP ≈ 43 VU/yr, **RDD ≈ 145°** (sand moves toward the SE/SSE), **RDP/DP ≈ 0.59**. Yearly DP ranges 48–94 VU; yearly RDD only **133–154°**. Exact values are in the tables above and in `wind_summary.json`.

**Which cell.** The box lies entirely in one native cell (22.75–23.25 N, 54.0625–54.6875 E); the centroid is 6.9 km from its western edge. The cell was confirmed hour-for-hour against NASA POWER at the centroid. Here the half-cell georeferencing error does matter: sampling Earth Engine at the centroid's own coordinates would read the cell to the **north** (centre 23.5 N), which for 2020–2024 gives DP 81.5 vs 69.1 VU (+18 %) and RDD 138.5° vs 146.1°. This cell is different from both Seih Al Hama's (24.0 N, 55.625 E) and Sweihan's (24.5 N, 55.625 E): **the three sites sample three different MERRA-2 cells.**

**Is the direction plausible?** Yes, and it is the clearest of the three sites. The regime is close to **unimodal**: about 73 % of DP comes from NNW–NW (337.5° alone is 46 %, 315° 25 %), the shamal and the daytime north-westerly, driving sand toward the SSE. About 20 % comes from the SSE–SSW sectors, driving sand north and reducing RDP; they seem to blow mainly in the early morning (around 03–07 UTC), where the hourly RDP/DP drops to 0.15–0.4 because the two modes oppose (an inference from the hour-of-day table, not a separate count). The resultant (145°) lies between the downwind directions of the two NW sectors (135° and 157.5°). May–July is the strongest and most unidirectional period (RDP/DP 0.68–0.95, RDD 141–152°). Every single year has RDD between 133° and 154°. This agrees with the published direction for Liwa: the draa migrate "very slowly to the south-southeast" (Pugh et al. 1993) and show "a slow advance to the south" (Lorenz & Radebaugh 2015).

**How far to trust it:** the same caveats as at the other sites. A ~55 × 64 km reanalysis of hourly means damps gusts, so absolute DP is likely biased low and the "low" class is approximate; RDD and RDP/DP are the more robust outputs. The cell is mostly open desert, so blending with towns is less of an issue than at Al Ain. A low DP does not contradict the large dunes: the megadunes are thousands of years old and move very slowly (~0.1 m/yr reported).

**NASA POWER** (2001–2024, same MERRA-2 source, so a processing check only): DP agrees to 0.001 % and RDD to 0.001° over the period; the worst year differs by 0.015 % / 0.008°. One hour (2004-09-05 17 UTC) differs, the same hour as at the other two sites, so it is a difference between the two MERRA-2 copies for that time step, not a site issue; it does not change DP.
