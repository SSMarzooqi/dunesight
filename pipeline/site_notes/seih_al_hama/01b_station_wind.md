## What the station winds say (written from the 2026-11-14 run; check the numbers above)

**Station.** Al Ain International (ISD 412180-99999, WMO 41218, ICAO OMAL), 24.262 N 55.609 E, 265 m, **24 km NNE of the box centre**. It reported hourly through all of 2000-2024: 89-100 % of hours each year, and every hour of the day within 1.4 percentage points of every other, so gaps cannot fake the diurnal or seasonal pattern. The station sits 1.3 km north of the MERRA-2 cell boundary, so its own cell (24.5 N) is the Sweihan cell, not the site cell (24.0 N); both are compared.

**Direction.** With exactly the notebook-01 definitions, the station's resultant drift points **north (RDD ≈ 9°)**, against 87° for MERRA-2 at the station's cell and 100° at the site cell. The measured dune heading (field median 29°) is **20° from the station RDD and 71° from MERRA-2's**. Station RDD is closer to the dune heading than MERRA-2's in 22 of 25 single years, and within 45° of it in 23 of 25.

**Why.** The two modes MERRA-2 shows are both present at the station and at about the same times of day, but their balance is reversed: the southerly mode (FROM 157.5-202.5°, peaking 05-07 UTC, 09-11 local) gives **55 % of the station's DP against 27 % in MERRA-2**, and the NW mode 22 % against 52 %. S/NW is 2.5 at the station and 0.5 in MERRA-2. Hour by hour, when both the station and MERRA-2 blow from the south the station is **35 % faster**; when both blow from the NW they agree to within 1 %. The southerly mode's DP is 3.9 times MERRA-2's, the NW mode's 0.8 times. So MERRA-2 gets the timing and direction of the southerly wind roughly right but underestimates its speed, and with DP ∝ ~V³ that flips which mode dominates. Scaling all station speeds down to MERRA-2's mean (×0.88) brings DP to 60 VU but leaves RDD at 3°: the direction difference is not a uniform speed bias.

**Is it just this anemometer?** Buraimi (WMO 41244, Oman, synoptic every 3 h; ISD places it 32.5 km ENE of the site, OSCAR 13 km further east since 2012, so its position is uncertain) has a very different speed climate (DP 67, 5.6 % calms) yet the same balance: S/NW 2.3 and RDD 341°. Two independent stations on the Al Ain-Buraimi plain agree on a southerly-dominated drift, so this is a regional feature MERRA-2 misses, not one instrument. Whether it reaches the dune field 24 km SSW of the airport and further from the mountains is **not tested**: there is no station inside the field.

**Amount.** Station DP is 132 VU/yr, **1.9 × MERRA-2 at its own cell and 2.0 × the site cell** (yearly ratio 1.4-2.6). It is still **low** (< 200) in Fryberger's classes; even +5 % on every speed only reaches 177. The handling choices move DP by less than 1 % (whole-knot recovery, SPECI, report window, calms, synoptic-only sampling, gap weighting) and RDD by under 2°. What matters is speed: ±5 % moves DP by −27 % / +34 %, and smoothing each hour with its neighbours (an upper bound on the 10-minute-vs-hourly-mean difference) lowers it by 26 % to 98 VU. Neither changes the direction by more than 5°.

**Does station wind explain the dune heading?** **Partly.** It removes most of the 71° disagreement: the observed southerly mode turns the expected drift to N-NNE, which matches the northern part of the box (moving N to NE) and the field median (29°) within 20°. It does **not** explain the southern belt moving east: at the station the only months with an eastward-to-south-eastward resultant are May-June and October-November (110-136°), and the only hours 10-15 UTC (70-156°), all from the NW mode and all outweighed over the year. That part remains unexplained by the station data; local steering by topography or a different balance of the two modes in the south of the box are possible but untested. The station is 24 km away, at 265 m on the foothill plain, so its winds are evidence about the regional regime, not a measurement in the dune field.
